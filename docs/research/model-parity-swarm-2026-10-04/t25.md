## Primary-source re-derivation
1. **Manifest integrity.** mos_NBS.parquet sha256 6f62a25a...7939 matches MANIFEST.json. All 176,594 rows have basis s3_lastmodified. Each runtime has exactly one availability value. IEM available_utc equals the listing LastModified on 697/697 runtimes.
2. **Live S3 ListObjectsV2.** I listed 31 history prefixes blend.YYYYMMDD/HH/text/: 30 random 00/06/12/18Z cycles from May to July (seed 20261004), plus 06-17 00Z. The LastModified matches the manifest to the second on 30/30.
   - 06-17 00Z has no text objects on S3 at all, although IEM has the bulletin. Excluding it, as the hunter did, is correct.
   - See s3_live_list_sample.json.
3. **Value identity, IEM vs the S3 object.**
   - I downloaded 8 history objects live: 05-02 12Z, 05-06 00Z, 05-20 18Z, 06-03 06Z, 06-18 12Z, 07-01 12Z, 07-09 00Z and 07-22 18Z. Each was about 30 MB, held in memory and never stored. I parsed them with A-NBH's own parser.
   - Across 88 station blocks, TMP/TSD/TXN/XND equal IEM's values on 2,024/2,024 rows, with 0 rows missing on either side. Every HTTP Last-Modified equals the manifest.
   - On the 268 overlapping 00/06/12/18Z cycles from 07-25 to 09-29 (S3 parts vs IEM), there are 0 mismatches on 67,804 TMP/TSD rows and 16,214 TXN/XND rows.
   - **Consequence:** IEM's copy carries exactly the content of the public S3 object. IEM's own ingest time (not exposed, and the IEM MOS page documents none) therefore does not matter: the same values were publicly downloadable from S3 at LastModified.
4. **Plausibility of the 36-90 min lags (T7's caveat).** The lags are stable over five months with a fixed per-cycle signature. The median lag is 42-55 min at 06Z and 18Z, and 75-100 min at 00Z and 12Z, where the full-depth runs take longer.
   - Nothing like a bulk backfill appears.
   - The outliers are late re-uploads: 05-04 13Z to 05-07 06Z, up to 1,936 min, and 07-01 12Z, 131 min. Using those later times is conservative.
   - LastModified is when the object became publicly downloadable. It cannot be earlier than real availability, and a NOAAPort/NOMADS copy could only have been earlier. The basis is therefore PIT-valid and, if anything, late.
   - The NCO production-status site lists no BLEND job, and I did not read NOMADS listings for current dates (>= 09-30, forbidden), so no schedule document was available. The measured times are the primary evidence.
5. **Version change found (not PIT).** The IEM MOS archive page (mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/mos/) states that NBS cycles changed from [1,7,13,19z] to [0,6,12,18z] on 2026-05-05. The bulletins confirm NBM V4.3 on 05-02 and V5.0 from 05-06. t6-r3's history window (05-01..07-31) therefore mixes about 5 days of v4.3 into a v5.0 fit. The v50_only variant below shows this is immaterial.
6. **Train/serve cadence mismatch (not leakage).** The history fit sees mainly the 4-per-day IEM cycles, while the evaluation sees hourly S3 cycles.
   - The selected cycle's median age at the simulated history snapshots is 4.0 h (p10/p90 2/6 h, n=22,451) for IEM cycles, and 1 h on the 07-25..07-31 S3 hourly days (n=1,826).
   - At evaluation it is 1.2-1.5 h.
   - Hourly NBS objects were public on S3 throughout the history window, so the IEM-only basis is *staler* than reality. That is PIT-safe, but it inflates the REM sigma scale slightly, as the +60/+120 trend shows.
   - An exact hourly history would need about 2,200 objects (about 66 GB). I did not fetch them.
