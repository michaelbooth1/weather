## 1. Primary-source evidence

### S3 (noaa-nbm-grib2-pds), re-measured 2026-10-04 02:30-02:41 local

Requests were anonymous HTTPS, sequential, at about 6 per second or fewer. T24 made two independent reads per cycle, for all 1,608 cycles (07-25..09-29, 00-23Z):
- one ListObjectsV2 of blend.YYYYMMDD/HH/text/;
- one HEAD of blend_nbstx.tHHz.

The results match the MANIFEST exactly:
- HEAD Last-Modified equals the MANIFEST s3_last_modified on 1608/1608 objects.
- The listing LastModified equals HEAD on 1608/1608.
- ETag matches on 1608/1608, and size matches on 1608/1608.

Facts about the objects:
- No multipart ETags (0/1608). Every object was a single PUT, so there is no "LastModified = multipart initiation" ambiguity. Any gap between upload start and finish for a single ~30 MB PUT is seconds, and the +5 min row count below covers it.
- The bucket is unversioned. ?versioning returns an empty configuration, and the 09-24 00Z object has VersionId=null. A re-upload therefore overwrites the earlier LastModified, which cannot be recovered. LastModified is the time of the LAST write, so it is never earlier than first availability on S3. That makes it conservative by construction.
- Lag after the cycle: min 36.5 min, median 44.3, p95 91.5, p99 136.3, max 857.2. None is negative and none is under 30 min.

Median lag by cycle hour (min):

| Cycle | 00 | 01 | 07 | 12 | 13 | 19 | all others |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Median lag | 78 | 65 | 77 | 96 | 64 | 77 | 40-49 |

This pattern matches the NWS service-change notices for NBM text messages, which say 01/07/13/19Z are disseminated later than the other cycles. The 00/12Z cycles are the full-depth runs.

Siblings. The 5 text bulletins (nbh, nbs, nbe, nbx, nbp) of a cycle land together:
- NBS minus the first sibling has a median of +83 s and a p95 of +368 s.
- The 09-24 00Z text set landed between 14:17:09 and 14:17:13.

### Whole-cycle listings (core/qmd/text) on 6 dates

The dates are 08-31, 09-14, 09-23 and 09-24, plus controls 08-10 and 09-05. They classify the 28 objects that are more than 30 min later than their cycle-hour median:
- Real upstream production delays (12 objects). The core GRIB was late by the same amount, so NOMADS was very likely late too, and LastModified is plausibly true first availability:
  - 08-31 12-14Z (core first 69-131 min);
  - 09-14 16-19Z (core first 107-153 min);
  - 09-23 19-21Z and 23Z (core first 74-142 min);
  - 09-24 13Z (core first 98 min).
- S3/NODD transfer backlog (13 objects, 09-24 00Z-12Z). All of core, qmd and text for these cycles were written in cycle order between 13:40Z and 16:10Z, with lags of 250-857 min. Meanwhile the newer 13Z cycle's text was already on S3 at 14:04Z, so cycles were published out of order. This is a transfer backlog, not a model delay, and those cycles were probably on NOMADS/NOAAPORT on time. No primary record of that survives: NOMADS keeps about 2 days, and the IEM AFOS archive holds no KWNO NBM text products (checked for 08-15). Rule 1 therefore keeps them at their S3 time, which is correct and conservative.
- 3 objects not classified (no whole-cycle listing for their dates): 07-30 01Z, 08-13 18Z and 09-25 01Z. Each is 31-63 min late, and its sibling text products were late too.

## 4. Implications

1. The NBS PIT is clean. No disqualifying defect. The T6 numbers stand as measured, and the attribution stands: the NBS-specific increment is null. The 17-23 gain is the decided-band/D-DEFECT family, and the 00-09 gain is the captured-NBM-v2 family.
2. Serving any NBS-based or NBM-text candidate (NBH as well):
   - A production poller of S3 adds a few minutes. At +5 min about 0.4% of snapshots get a different distribution, so evaluation should use LastModified plus the poll interval.
   - Polling NOMADS instead would have avoided the 09-24 backlog. This is a design note only, not a proposal.
3. Canon note for the synthesis. "Late re-uploads" should read "upstream delays (08-31, 09-14, 09-23) plus one S3 transfer backlog (09-24 00-12Z)". S3 LastModified is last-write time on an unversioned bucket, so it is an upper bound on first S3 availability.
