## Primary source and method

Source: the aviationweather.gov Data API (/api/data/metar?format=json; public NWS AWC, no key). Each report carries receiptTime, the AWC ingest time to the millisecond, beside obsTime. This is the per-object timestamp that earlier refuters could not obtain from IEM:
- refute-pit-t1 found no MTR products in the AFOS archive.
- asos.py has no ingest time.
- refute-pit-t15 assumed AWC's retention lay entirely at or after 09-30.

In fact AWC still held 2026-09-04 00:56Z..09-29 at fetch time, so the window is about 25 days and 7,672 reports.

Fetch: 88 requests at 2 s spacing, in 96 h pages ending at 2026-09-29T23:30Z and paged back until HTTP 400 (end of retention). Any record with obsTime or receiptTime on or after 2026-09-30T00:00Z was dropped in memory before writing: 0 dropped, none stored. Raw JSON with sha256 is in C:\swarm\out\t26\raw\ with MANIFEST.json.

Code:
- C:\pt\swarm\tools\research\model_parity\t26_fetch_awc.py
- t26_metar_pit.py (lag, matching, row impact)
- t26_t1_receipt_sensitivity.py

Matching: IEM rows were joined to AWC non-COR reports on station and valid time, taking the earliest AWC receipt.

Caveat: AWC receipt is one public endpoint. NOAAPort/LDM (IEM) or the NWS TGFTP feed may differ by about a minute. The production-capture witness in refute-pit-t2 and r-pit-t3 is independent evidence (92-93% reflected at 10-15 min; Denver the outlier at 78.9%), and it agrees with these measurements, including the Denver anomaly.

## 2. Value integrity (truth and history-fit use)

Rows compared: in the window, IEM has 7,700 rows: 7,523 non-COR, of which 7,493 match an AWC original. The 30 unmatched rows are mostly at the window edges, plus 3 KBKF and 1 KAUS mid-window. AWC has 0 valid times that are absent from IEM.

Values: on every matched row, IEM's temperature in F (T-group, half-up rounding) equals AWC's real-time value: 7,493 of 7,493, 100%. Raw text is equal on 99.99%. There is no sign of post-hoc revision of tmpf or T-group in the IEM archive for this window.

COR handling: IEM holds 177 COR rows. AWC still has the uncorrected original for only 6 of them, so both archives keep the corrected report. For about 2.3% of hours the original real-time value is therefore unrecoverable. The hunters' COR exclusion leaves those rows as a gap, which is conservative, as they disclosed.

Truth and labels: timing does not matter for the label use. Value agreement is complete, and F2's RS_ct matches the winner on 626/626 market-days.

History fits (<= 2026-07-31): these build PIT states with the same +10 basis. AWC history does not reach that far. If the KBKF lag profile also held in history, the KBKF history states are about as optimistic as the live ones, and the effect is bounded by the refuters' "both-shift" +60 and +120 runs.

## 5. Implications (no new rules)

- The +10 min basis is a fair, mostly conservative proxy except at KBKF. Refuters' +60 and +120 runs bound every station except a small KBKF tail.
- Any 13-16 or morning claim that leans on KBKF rows should be read with the KBKF lag in mind. r-pit-t3 already called the 13-16 LEAD availability-sensitive.
- For drafting only (not a rule): a future pre-registration or production capture should record a per-report receipt time, for example AWC receiptTime or the production ingest stamp, instead of a constant offset. A constant offset is wrong for military and augmented stations.
