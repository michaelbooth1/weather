## Rules (registered in C:\swarm\registry.jsonl before the first score; agent t17; 3 entries)
- **t17-r1 (candidate).** METAR fast floor. R is today's PIT running max of IEM METAR. It uses routine and SPECI reports,
  excludes COR, converts T-group C to F with half-up rounding, and takes a row only when valid + 10 min <= captured_at.
  Served probabilities on bands entirely below R are set to 0 and the rest renormalised, then the harness floor is
  applied. With no METAR today the snapshot falls back to served. The rule has zero parameters and no hour gate.
- **t17-c0 (control).** Served plus the harness floor only. This is the no-source control and equals the floor-only rung.
- **t17-d1 (diagnostic, not a candidate).** The event-centred step study described below.
  The event windows are located using events after t, so it is scoreboard-only and never a candidate input.

Multiplicity contribution: 1 candidate, 1 control and 1 diagnostic.

## Point-in-time statement
- **t17-r1 inputs.** The only inputs are METAR rows with available_utc = valid + 10 min (the A-IEM-1 basis) <= captured_at_utc.
  h.assert_point_in_time is called on every joined row. COR reports are excluded. There is no market field, no label and

## Part A: event-centred step study (t17-d1)
**Method (F3, extended).**
- Each snapshot is attached to the earliest event of its station and date with x = t - V in [-30, +30) min.
- The residual is the snapshot value minus that event window's mean.
- The step is the mean over [+5, +25) for METAR, or [0, +20) for the other events, minus the mean over [-20, 0).
- The placebo shifts every event by +30 min. For hourly events the placebo is contaminated by the next METAR, so
  the cleanest contrast is the difference between informative and uninformative events (DiD) at the same clock positions.
- Intervals come from W over market-day cells.

**Informative events:**
- METAR: a new running max for the day.
- NBH: the max TMP over the target-day hours still ahead changed by >= 1 F against the previous cycle, over the same hours.
- HRRR, NBM v2 and NWS: |change| >= 1 F.

**Event counts** (target dates in the table): METAR 14,640 (3,396 informative); NBH 29,373 station-cycle-dates (1,728
informative); HRRR 4,930 (1,484); NBM v2 641 (265, all before 13:00 local); NWS 4,233 (571).

## Serveability
- **t17-r1:** zero-parameter serving stage. It is not worth serving. Production already ingests METAR into high_so_far
  about 4 min after the valid time, and the measured increment is -0.00005.
- **Study:** nothing to serve. The market is studied as an object only.
- **Implications:**
  - **Q-07 (withdraw signal).** On T+0, the market's information clock is the METAR clock, with dp peaking at +8 to +14
    min after the valid time. NBH, HRRR, NBM and NWS publication times are not repricing moments. A METAR-clock pull
    window of roughly valid-2 to valid+15 min is the descriptive shape. It needs a forward pre-registration (89a/88a); it
    is not a finding here.
  - **Q-02.** There is no evidence that guidance publication is an information event for these markets. The METAR clock
    is.
  - **Q-06.** The market's repricing at METARs is not instantaneous (median movement 8-14 min after the valid time), and
    served is earlier.

## Limitations
- Snapshot cadence is about 9.1 min, so the onset curve's resolution comes from the spread of event offsets, not from
  dense sampling.
- Market quote times are the captured_at times; the books may be a few seconds stale.
- Hourly events of different kinds sit at fixed clock offsets: routine METAR at :51-:56, NBH LastModified around :40. Only
  the DiD contrasts are free of that confound.
- Informative classes for NBH, HRRR and NWS use a >= 1 F threshold fixed a priori and not tuned.
- The 17-23 informative-METAR cells are small, with wide intervals.
