## Headline for hunters

1. **Past sunset is the single largest cell: 20.0% of all before-stratum excess.** In 17-23, once local
   sunset has passed, served keeps **0.377** of its mass on bands above the PIT METAR running-max band.
   The market keeps 0.012, and 0.009 of these snapshots settled above. Every one of the 11 markets has
   positive excess in this cell, from Miami at 0.6% to Los Angeles at 4.3% of total excess. Almost all
   of it falls on EF tail rows: 14.8% of all positive tail excess. Market Brier there is 0.0000.
2. **The rest of 17-23 has the same shape before sunset.** Snapshots 3°F or more below the running max
   hold 6.4% of total excess, 1-2°F below hold 4.9%, and at the max hold 3.7%. Served P(above the
   running-max band) is 0.34-0.47 against market 0.01-0.08 (realised 0.01-0.03). 17-23 in total:
   **35.0%** of excess. Of that, 20.9% of total excess sits on the band containing the running max,
   which served under-weights, and 13.9% on the bands above it. That is the T1 target, and it is mass
   held on the wrong side of the floor, not floor leakage.
3. **13-16: served over-weights "above" only once the temperature is off the max.** Off-max 1-2°F:
   served P(above) 0.44, market 0.16, realised 0.13. Off-max 3°F or more: 0.42 / 0.13 / 0.17. At max
   and rising, served 0.65, market 0.65 and realised 0.66 agree. There the excess is 8.9% of total
   and comes from band sharpness, not from the above/below split. 13-16 in total: 14.8%.
4. **10-12 at-max-rising (85% of 10-12 snapshots) holds 9.9% of excess**, mostly on above bands
   (8.3%). Served P(above) 0.84 vs market 0.96 vs realised 0.94: served is *too low* on "above" here.
   It leaves mass in or below the running-max band while the day is still warming.
5. **00-09 (39.6% of excess) is spread across states with no dominant one.** It is not an obs-state
   problem: served P(above) 0.75-0.93 against market and realised about 0.97-1.0. Off-max 3°F or more
   (the midnight carry-over is the running max) is the worst in 06-09: 8.5% of total, served 0.75 vs
   realised 0.97.
   Caveat: before about 10:00 the METAR running max is often last evening's warmth (see the
   high_so_far note below), so in 00-09 "band position" is weak.

## Market information arrival (before stratum; market studied as an object only)

The question: does market Brier drop when a routine METAR becomes available? (Snapshot cadence is
9.1 min in every market.)

- **Naive curve, Brier vs minutes since the latest routine METAR: do not use it.** Routine METARs
  come at :51-:56, so minutes-since is almost exactly minute-of-hour. Its within-hour residual
  (early minus late, +0.00076 [+0.00042, +0.00112]) is a clock effect.
- **Event-centred step test.** Residual within each (date, market, METAR event) window; step = mean
  over t - valid in [5,25) min minus [-20,0). Placebo = the same at the half hour.

| 17-23 | -.0003 [-.0008, -.0000] | +.0002 | -.0003 [-.0021,.0017] | +.0001 [-.0019,.0020] |

Read: in 13-16 the market's Brier has a clear sawtooth. It drops about 0.004 within about 25 min
after each routine METAR valid time and climbs back before the next one (placebo +0.0036). The curve
in `f3_event_curve_before.parquet` falls monotonically from -10 to +25 min.
**Served drops as much or more at the same METARs**, so the served-market excess has no significant
step anywhere. The before stratum gives no evidence that the market reacts to METARs faster than
served does. The excess is a level gap, not a reaction-time gap. Elsewhere the steps are tiny.
**Caveat on timing:** the drop starts before valid + 10 min, the A-IEM availability assumption.
Markets, and possibly served, may receive the observation earlier, or may use 1-/5-minute data.
That question is for T17. These are within-hour descriptives, not causal.

## Notes and traps

- **The METAR running max differs from the captured `high_so_far` in the morning.** In 00-09 the
  METAR running max (since local midnight, routine + SPECI, non-COR) is above `high_so_far` by
  +1.5 to +1.8°F on average (>= 1°F in 41-52% of snapshots). By 17-23 they agree (mean -0.01°F,
  1.6% differ by >= 1°F). Production's `high_so_far` evidently starts later than local midnight or
  ignores the midnight carry-over. Hunters building "decided" rules should use METAR from the
  morning onward, or state which definition they use.
- The obs state is point in time: METAR/SPECI with available_utc (valid + 10 min) <= captured_at_utc,
  COR reports excluded, local date = target date. `h.assert_point_in_time` passed for every station.
  The trend is current minus the last obs valid at least 45 min earlier (`na` if that obs is more
  than 120 min old). Rising means >= +1°F, falling <= -1°F. Sunset is computed with the NOAA solar
  equation (zenith 90.833°) at the station coordinates in `stations.json`.
- State precedence: no_obs_today -> past_sunset -> off_max_3plus (deficit >= 3°F) -> off_max_1_2 ->
  at_max_rising -> at_max_flat_or_falling.
- The event-step test uses METARs after t to locate the event window. This is acceptable for a
  scoreboard descriptive and **must never be copied into a candidate.**
